# Market sizing: source counts, assumptions and the first reachable segment

Prepared and sources checked: **30 October 2026**. All amounts are USD per year
unless stated otherwise. This is a bottom-up scenario model for diligence, not
measured customer demand, a valuation, a forecast or a claim of global market
coverage. No price or screening rate below has been validated with buyers.

## The buying unit

The first offer serves a software engineering team or coding-agent integrator
reviewing agent-generated changes in one authorized repository task family.
Count the organization with the purchasing budget once, not every developer,
repository, agent, model, office or subsidiary brand. Additional task families
are expansion within that account; they are not additional organizations.

The current recurring-service price hypothesis is $1,000 per month, or **$12,000
for a full year**, for a bounded workload. This is service pricing to test, not a
validated SaaS contract value. The separate $5,000 pilot is excluded from annual
recurring market calculations. Price acceptance, delivery effort and repeat use
must be observed before assuming a software subscription model. See
[commercial plan](COMMERCIAL_PLAN.md) and [financial plan](FINANCIAL_PLAN.md).

## International TAM reference: quantify one defined segment first

The intended long-term market is organizations that need recurring verification
of coding-agent releases. Its worldwide size has **not** been measured. A
verifiable U.S. software-publisher population provides one bounded reference:

`reference annual spend = 16,824 firms × eligible buying share × annual price`

“Eligible buying share” is an explicit assumption: firms must have a relevant
recurring coding-agent workflow, a release owner, authorized usable evidence,
an unmet need relative to their existing checks, and a plausible budget for this
offer. It is not an observed AI-adoption percentage or a sales-conversion rate.

| Assumed eligible share of the U.S. reference | $6,000/year price sensitivity | $12,000/year current hypothesis | $24,000/year price sensitivity |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5% | $5,047,200 | $10,094,400 | $20,188,800 |
| 10% | $10,094,400 | **$20,188,800** | $40,377,600 |
| 20% | $20,188,800 | $40,377,600 | $80,755,200 |

At the central 10% assumption, the arithmetic represents about 1,682 eligible
account equivalents; no such account list has been established. Calculations use
the unrounded population-share product. The 5–20% range is a sensitivity test,
not a statistical confidence interval or evidence that the true share lies in
that range. The $6,000 and $24,000 prices are sensitivities, not new offers.

At 100% of firms and $12,000 each, the **gross reference ceiling is $201,888,000**.
This is deliberately an unscreened ceiling. Software publishers include firms
outside the product's actual buyer definition. The ceiling is **not verified
qualified TAM**, not the worldwide TAM, and not a revenue target. Other software
services, countries and enterprise engineering teams are potential extensions
to investigate; they have not been added to inflate this number.

## Qatar entry SAM: a provisional serviceability model

The initial Qatar opportunity uses a different source population and geography:

`provisional Qatar SAM = 409 directory listings × screened serviceable share × $12,000`

The assumed share must absorb exclusions for duplicate purchasing groups,
irrelevant ICT activity, lack of a recurring coding-agent workflow, inaccessible
test evidence, adequate existing tools, absent budget, and an infeasible
procurement or delivery route. It is not measured merely by finding AI on a
company website. Serviceability also depends on the Lab establishing its own
legal, security and delivery arrangements.

| Assumed screened serviceable share | Account equivalents, before rounding | Annual spend at $12,000 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 5% | 20.45 | $245,400 |
| 10% | 40.90 | **$490,800** |
| 20% | 81.80 | $981,600 |

For the central 10% share, price sensitivity is $245,400 at $6,000/year and
$981,600 at $24,000/year. These outputs are modeled equivalents, not counts of
qualified prospects. If screening finds only five relevant procurable accounts,
the same $12,000 assumption yields $60,000; if none qualifies, the reachable
opportunity for this initial offer is zero. The model must be allowed to fail.

This Qatar entry SAM and the U.S. reference are **separate geographic scenarios**.
Qatar is not a subset of the U.S. Census population. Do not depict them as nested
circles, subtract one from the other, sum them as a global total, or claim a
Qatar share of the U.S. reference market. A properly reconciled worldwide TAM
with a nested Qatar SAM requires more source and account-level work.

## Obtainable sales: capacity and purchase evidence govern the first year

The [financial model](FINANCIAL_PLAN.md) supplies the consistent first-year
planning cases: nine completed pilot invoices plus 16 recurring customer-months
produce **$61,000 invoiced revenue and $52,000 cash collections** under its base
assumptions. Its downside is $16,000 invoiced and $10,000 collected. These are
scenario outputs with distinct timing, not booked business or a market-share
forecast. Customer-months are summed over actual proposed start dates; they do
not imply 16 distinct customers or a full year of recurring revenue per buyer.

The current validation target is 15 buyer interviews, three assisted staging
installations and one signed paid scope or budget-authorized LOI in the first
90 days. These targets are not achieved results; an LOI is not payment. The
model's first completed pilot invoice remains in month 4. Use signed scopes,
completed work, actual support hours, invoices and receipts to update obtainable
sales rather than taking an arbitrary percentage of TAM.

## How to replace assumptions with evidence

1. Freeze a dated country/industry definition and retain each account's source,
   legal/brand identity, parent-group relationship and exclusion reason.
2. Screen the CRA directory by application/software-development activity, then
   resolve whether the team actually uses coding agents for changes it releases.
   AI chatbots or data-science services alone do not establish that fit.
3. Qualify the release owner, authorized test repository, recurring task family,
   budget owner, current checks and feasible buying path. Preserve rejected
   accounts and all unknown fields. A named research target is not a qualified
   opportunity until those fields are supported.
4. Ask for a priced scope and measure delivery effort. Separate stated interest,
   accepted price, signed agreement, completed work, invoice and collected cash.
5. Recalculate by actual qualified purchasing account; record uncertainty and
   the source date. Add countries or industries only after resolving parent,
   reseller and customer overlap. Count an integrator contract or the end-customer
   contract according to who buys, not both for the same budget.
