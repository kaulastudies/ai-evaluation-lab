# Investment case: pay for a decision, then fund repeatability

Status: a proposed investment argument for an initial **$12,000 validation allocation**, 30 October 2026. It draws on the [financial plan](FINANCIAL_PLAN.md) and [model output](../../programs/startup-qatar/financials.json). No customer revenue, funding award, historical account balance or agreed investment terms are established here. The operating-cost assumptions remain illustrative; later funding amounts are unpriced.

## The case a selector could support

AI Evaluation Lab has a technical starting point that can be inspected. The commercial question is still open: will an engineering team pay for better evidence that its agent completed an authorised action correctly? An initial allocation could answer that question with a bounded cost and a clear stop decision.

The first stage should produce 15 buyer interviews, three assisted staging installations and one signed paid scope or budget-authorised letter of intent, as specified in the current [commercial plan](COMMERCIAL_PLAN.md). These are targets. An installation is not a customer purchase, a letter of intent is not cash, and the financial model conservatively puts the first completed pilot invoice in month 4.

A selector should require a buyer-defined workflow, a fair comparison with its existing checks, measured adoption effort and an independently reviewed result. Evidence of an additional catch or lower review effort matters only if the buyer accepts the trade-off and a plausible purchase route exists. The [pilot protocol](PILOT_PROTOCOL.md) defines the measurements and failure rules.

This makes a small initial allocation defensible. It does not yet justify a large expansion investment. If interviews show no material problem, staging adoption is too expensive, or the buyer's current tooling is adequate, the result should end or redirect the plan before hiring and establishment costs are committed.

## A tighter first-stage option

The model's scheduled cash costs for months 1–3 are:

|---|---:|
| Founder draw: three months × $2,000 | $6,000 |
| Infrastructure: three months × $400 | $1,200 |
| Independent review scheduled in months 2 and 3 | $3,000 |
| Scheduled cash cost | **$10,200** |
| 15% contingency on those costs | $1,530 |
| Cost plus contingency | **$11,730** |

The current proposal is **$12,000 for the initial validation stage**, subject to confirmed eligible costs and the founder's actual availability. The $270 rounding headroom is explicit. This is a proposed allocation for negotiation, not an approved programme tranche or commitment to spend.

The earlier $20,000 example contained $8,270 beyond the cost-plus-contingency amount and is superseded as the recommended first request. A larger amount needs itemised evidence of necessary working capital. The $12,000 does not cover an unquoted requirement for immediate Qatar establishment or relocation. If such a requirement applies before validation, obtain its costs and revise the scope before submission. Confirm that the programme accepts the proposed allocation size and milestone structure; that is not yet known.

## Explain the $100,000 ceiling honestly

| Modelled situation | What it says about capital |
|---|---|
| No-collection stress envelope | $85,550 scheduled operating costs + $12,832.50 contingency = $98,382.50, rounded to a $100,000 planning ceiling. It assumes work is funded even if customer cash never arrives. |
| Base execution hypothesis | $61,000 invoiced, $52,000 collected and $85,550 cash cost imply a peak incremental cash requirement of $33,550 from a modelled zero opening balance. Customer assumptions remain unvalidated. |
| Full ceiling plus base collections | $100,000 + $52,000 − $85,550 = $66,450 year-end cash. This is a reason to reduce later releases, not a case for spending the balance. |
| Initial request only | $12,000 + $52,000 − $85,550 = negative $21,550 year-end cash if the whole base operating plan continues. Both scenarios first need additional funding in month 4. Stop or re-scope before that point unless a separately costed follow-on is approved and received. |
| Downside hypothesis | $10,000 collected against $79,550 cash costs creates a $69,550 peak incremental cash requirement if spending continues. Actual failure gates should stop that continuation earlier. |

The $100,000 is an upper planning envelope, not an upfront request or a budget that must be exhausted. The earlier $20,000 / $30,000 / $50,000 financing schedule is superseded. Current follow-on reviews are proposed for months 4 and 7 with amounts deliberately unset. After any initial award, negotiate the next amount from the remaining approved work, measured costs and funds already available. Do not subtract a hoped-for customer payment as though it were cash on hand. Do remove a cost when it is already funded by an award or a confirmed credit specifically applicable to it.

At each release decision use: remaining approved cash costs + agreed liquidity reserve − cash actually available for that scope − confirmed cost offsets. Reconcile timing as well as the total. Count a non-cash credit only against the eligible expense it can reduce. A negative result means no additional release is needed. Separate balances restricted to other work must not be treated as freely available cash.

## How a larger opportunity could emerge

The first offer is a service that tests one workflow. A larger business needs evidence that the same contract, connector and report can be reused across buyers with limited custom work. The [platform strategy](PLATFORM_STRATEGY.md) describes the proposed direction; it is not a claim of existing platform revenue.

| Evidence stage | Commercial result needed | Funding decision it could support |
|---|---|---|
| First buyer | Accepted evaluation result, measured hours and a paid scope or documented purchase process | Complete the bounded delivery and validate collection timing |
| Second independent buyer | The same core workflow approach works with measured reuse and acceptable integration effort | Fund the repeated component instead of a broad feature list |
| Paid continuation | Buyer pays to rerun checks for a new release; actual recurring effort fits the scope | Support a repeatable service and a carefully scoped local engineering role |
| Several retained buyers | Renewal, support effort, collections and acquisition cost are measured over time | Reassess software investment and larger distribution, using observed economics |

The $5,000 pilot currently has an assumed 25% contribution before overhead and tax. The proposed $1,000 monthly service has an assumed 50% contribution at eight delivery hours and $100 compute. Those are hypotheses with little room for uncontrolled custom work. A larger valuation or market claim would be premature until paid repetition demonstrates why revenue can grow faster than delivery effort. No investor return, market share or acceptance probability is estimated here.

Qatar's value would be a useful place to validate the workflow with local buyers, develop relevant implementation expertise and, when justified, create a funded local role. Entity registration alone does not establish that value. Bilingual evaluation belongs in the plan only when buyers request it and qualified reviewers can validate it.

## What must be ready for submission versus investment execution

| Before a truthful submission | Before terms, disbursement or spending |
|---|---|
| Identify the applicant and legal status accurately. Reconcile the requested historical reporting period from records, or explicitly explain unavailable history. | Confirm the investment instrument, dilution and investor rights in writing. |
| Separate product revenue, collected cash, invoices, grants and founder contributions. Explain the forecast assumptions and preserve unknowns. | Obtain itemised entity, visa, employment, tax, insurance, workspace and relocation costs relevant to the chosen route. |
| State the actual founder availability and honest answers about Qatar presence and hiring. Do not substitute this plan for consent. | Establish contracting and banking authority, permitted cost categories, milestone evidence and the cash-release timetable. |
| Include the projected financial attachment and the evidence-backed product case with its limitations. | Resolve residency and proof-of-funds requirements, if applicable, and confirm adequate available cash before commitments. |

An unknown item cannot be marked complete. Some execution details may be settled during diligence, but a required application answer must either be truthful and supported or clearly unresolved where the form permits it. Check the programme's actual requirements before submission; this document does not invent a rule that all quotations must be purchased in advance.

Use the [reconciliation template](FINANCIAL_RECONCILIATION_TEMPLATE.csv) in a **private copy**. The public file is deliberately blank. `UNKNOWN` means unverified, not zero. Enter a zero only after records for a defined period support it. Store only private evidence references in the completed copy and do not commit financial records, bank statements or personal identifiers to this repository.

The existing public-research funding request remains separate. Allocate shared costs once, keep restricted funds with their purpose, and never treat unrelated personal or legacy task payments as Lab product revenue.
