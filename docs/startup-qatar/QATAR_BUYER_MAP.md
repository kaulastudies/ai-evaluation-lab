# Qatar buyer research map

Prepared and primary sources retrieved: **30 October 2026**. These are ten named
research targets, not contacts, customers, partners, scheduled meetings or
qualified sales opportunities. No outreach has been sent through this work.
Public relevance does not establish coding-agent adoption, need, budget or
willingness to buy. No company logo or reference may be used as an endorsement.

## Why these accounts

The initial offer is release verification for coding-agent changes in one
authorized repository task family. Favor an engineering team or integrator that
can provide a synthetic/staging repository, an accountable release owner and a
repeatable code-review decision. The support/approval workflow is a separate
expansion prototype, not the first customer product.

The first research sequence is iHorizons, malomatia, Mannai and Advancya: their
published development or integration work is relevant to discovering such an
engineering owner. This is a prioritization judgment, not evidence of purchase
intent or permission to contact anyone. Four other technology providers warrant
qualification. Two larger operators provide adjacent evidence of AI adoption,
but their procurement and integration burden may make them poor first customers.

## Qualification before an account becomes a sales opportunity

For each account, keep a private, dated qualification record with sources and
clear `confirmed`, `rejected` or `unknown` states:

| Required field | Evidence that advances the account | Current state for these targets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchasing identity | Legal entity, brand/parent relation and contracting authority | Names are research labels; four public registration fields are noted, but purchasing groups are unreconciled |
| Relevant workflow | Engineering owner confirms recurring agent-generated changes in a specified repository task family | Unknown |
| Decision and pain | A specific release, missing check, regression or costly review process, with source of any time/cost estimate | Unknown |
| Existing alternative | Actual tests, CI/evaluation process, owners and setup/maintenance effort | Unknown |
| Authorized access | Written permission for synthetic/staging artifacts, isolation, IP and retention boundaries | Unknown |
| Champion and budget | Named engineering owner and person who can approve a priced scope | Unknown |
| Adoption feasibility | Required security/procurement reviews, contracting route and plausible timetable | Unknown |
| Commercial next step | Accepted scope/price, written conditions and decision date | Unknown |

Public fit alone does not advance these fields. The ten-account list is not a
ten-customer pipeline, and it does not establish the assumed 5–20% serviceable
share in [market sizing](MARKET_SIZING.md). That share is explicitly a sensitivity
input until broader screening and buyer evidence replace it.

## One bounded discovery request to prepare

Proposed discussion scope: a 20-minute conversation with the engineering owner
about how AI-generated changes are currently accepted. Ask for one recent
decision, existing checks and review effort before showing the demonstration.
If there is a genuine gap, propose a synthetic or authorized staging task and
measure the Lab against the current process. Do not ask for customer production
code, credentials or private incident details during initial discovery.

For a qualified buyer, the proposed next step is a bounded $5,000 pilot scope for
one task family, with independent cases and a fair baseline, subject to actual
price acceptance and access. The recurring $1,000/month hypothesis is discussed
only when a repeat release need exists. No quote or commitment has been issued.

The common first-90-day validation targets are **15 buyer interviews, three
assisted staging installations and one signed paid scope or budget-authorized
LOI**. Fifteen interviews can include multiple roles at one organization; report
distinct organizations separately and do not call 15 interviews 15 buyers.
These ten research names are a starting list, not an assurance that targets can
be reached. A LOI is weaker than payment, and a staging installation is not
completed paid delivery. The financial model's first completed pilot invoice
remains in month 4.

## Counting and publication rules

- Use the purchasing organization as the account key. Link multiple brands,
  business units, directory entries and contact roles to it rather than counting
  them again. The map's ordering is provisional until ownership is reconciled.
- Count Mannai Technologies and Mannai InfoTech together here. Do not turn a
  listed integrator's customers into the Lab's reachable accounts or partnerships.
- Do not add these ten names to the CRA directory's 409. Some may already appear
  in it; complete overlap has not been checked. The map also includes research
  targets found through company publications rather than a directory sample.
- A reseller/prime-contractor opportunity and its end customer may share one
  procurement budget. Count the prospective payer once for that scope; document
  separate purchases before treating both as independent revenue opportunities.
- Recheck a page before using its current claim. Publication date, retrieval date
  and an undated page's status are different fields. A search engine's crawl date
  is not a publication date.
- Keep interview identities, correspondence and private qualification evidence
  outside the public repository. Publish names or testimonials as customer proof
  only with both factual support and the necessary permission.
