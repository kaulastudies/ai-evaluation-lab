# Evidence-to-Governance: A Focused Study of Repository-Agent Verification

**Research outline — version 0.1 (31 October 2026)**

**Author:** Rama Chandra M, AI Evaluation Lab

**ORCID:** https://orcid.org/0009-0008-0189-2166

**Status:** Source-based retrospective case selection plus a **proposed prospective human-review study**; no reviewer outcomes have been collected, and the protocol is not yet preregistered. This is not a peer-reviewed paper, independent audit, safety certification, or deployment approval.

**Study materials:** [Prospective protocol draft v0.2](PROTOCOL_DRAFT_v0.2.md) | [Evidence version register](EVIDENCE_VERSION_REGISTER.md) | [Reviewer case-sheet template](REVIEWER_CASE_SHEET_TEMPLATE.md)

## Research question

**When AI-generated repository changes pass ordinary public tests but fail independent, qualified verification, what governance decisions can be supported by the preserved evidence—and what decisions remain unjustified?**

This study deliberately does *not* ask which AI model is safest overall. It asks how a bounded, reproducible engineering evaluation can inform a human review of whether to accept, rework, hold, or reject a specific code change under a stated task contract.

## Retrospective cases and candidate governance implications

| Evidence observed | Narrow conclusion justified | Human decision it may support | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AP-005**: candidate passed public tests; qualified verifier rejected four edge-case gates | Public tests alone were insufficient for this task instance | Hold merge; request bounded repair; reverify on the *same frozen criteria* | That all other public-test passes are unsafe |
| **AP-005 repair**: evidence-bounded repair passed qualified gates; parent failure preserved | A repaired candidate satisfied the specified contract in this bounded run | Consider acceptance *only for that defined task and environment*, subject to project review | General code safety or deployment approval |
| **AP-003**: public tests passed but duplicate-processing checks failed; repair budget ended without conversion | The failure persisted under the configured verifier and repair budget | Reject/hold change and escalate to a human maintainer | The model's overall inability to solve the class of problem |
| **Admission HOLDs**: candidate output rejected before evaluation | No executed-candidate verdict was established | Request a properly formatted submission; avoid counting the HOLD as verified success | That the generated implementation was functionally defective |
| **AP-001 repeated trials**: 29/30 passes across three fixed configurations; all 30 replayed | The committed AP-001 outcomes are reproducible at the recorded source state | Flag evidence sufficiency and demand broader task testing before wider reliance | Ranking all coding agents or predicting production incidents |
| **17/17 configured mutants detected** | The particular verifier qualification controls worked in the documented runs | Increase confidence in the gate *for these configured faults* | Exhaustive verifier correctness, security or isolation |

**Decision terminology:** “accept” here is a *researcher's hypothetical code-review disposition*, not a production deploy decision. Real deployments also require security review, system-specific risk assessment, authorization, monitoring, rollback readiness, and a named human accountable for approval.

## Proposed method (not yet executed as a standalone governance study)

1. **Freeze the source:** record commit SHAs, task contracts, verifier versions, immutable evidence digests, report version, and the 52-attempt snapshot. Retain the older 51-attempt October report separately. The [version register](EVIDENCE_VERSION_REGISTER.md) records the discrepancy but per-case artifact hashes and a fresh replay are still pending.
2. **Predefine a decision rubric:** for each candidate identify public-test status, admission, verifier qualification, verifier result, replay state, known uncertainty, permissible review decision, human owner, and missing evidence.
3. **Blind initial assessment:** prepare leakage-checked baseline packets for AP-005 before repair, AP-005 after repair, AP-003 terminal failure, a HOLD, and an AP-001 repeated-run subset; control for related-case exposure. Ask independent reviewers to choose *accept / repair / hold / reject / cannot determine*, give a reason and record confidence before the evidence reveal. Preserve those baseline answers without editing.
4. **Reveal independent-verification evidence:** present the additional gates and linked source-exact records; record whether and why the recommended decision changes. Any inter-reviewer agreement would be newly measured, not inferred from existing RARB experiments.
5. **Score the adequacy, not just the change:** freeze an evidence-adequacy rubric and defensible alternatives before seeing participant responses; retain initial independent judgments, disagreements and adjudication. For every conclusion distinguish (a) technical signal, (b) demonstrated bounded reliability, and (c) governance disposition that still requires contextual human authorization.
6. **Report negative findings and limitations:** expose disagreement and cases in which the evidence cannot support a disposition; publish machine-readable decision sheets and methods, subject to privacy/source-access checks.

**Primary proposed study outcome:** proportion of human-review dispositions that satisfy a **case-specific, independently frozen evidence-adequacy rubric** before versus after verifier evidence is revealed. **A changed decision is not necessarily a better decision.** Decision change, appropriately qualified/abstained decisions, confidence, agreement and justification are separate secondary/descriptive outcomes. None has yet been measured. The complete protocol, case selection, conflict criteria, freeze procedure and data handling remain draft until finalized before reviewer recruitment; see [protocol v0.2](PROTOCOL_DRAFT_v0.2.md).

## Threats to validity / exclusions

- Synthetic repository tasks, non-random attempt selection, single-task Phase 12 model comparison, and evolving task/evidence versions.
- Verifier qualification covers a configured mutant set; an unknown fault may still escape.
- The researcher's design of the cases and decision rubric may influence outcomes. Independent reviewer assessment has *not* yet been conducted.
- No external organizational risk appetite, developer authorization, production telemetry, or real-user incident measurements are represented.
- A security-hardening change is currently proposed in [PR #34](https://github.com/kaulastudies/ai-evaluation-lab/pull/34) and must not be treated as merged into main or independently security-certified.
- A separate [Evercraft/EO-001 receipt audit draft](https://github.com/kaulastudies/ai-evaluation-lab/pull/30) explores signed-evidence/authority mismatches, but is **not included in the 52-attempt RARB dataset** and is not required to reproduce this study.

## Intended contribution

A small, inspectable *evidence-to-decision protocol* that prevents benchmark-style successes from being mistaken for broad reliability or operational authorization. The output is meant for critique and replication by independent researchers. Any future use in a governance or standards process would require separate technical review, approvals, and attribution.
